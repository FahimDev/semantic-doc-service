# Security Policy

## Multi-factor authentication

Enable multi-factor authentication on every supported service. Hardware security keys are preferred, and authenticator apps are acceptable. SMS codes are allowed only as a last-resort fallback because phone numbers can be hijacked.

## Password management

Store every password in the company password manager and generate unique random passwords of at least 20 characters. Never reuse a password across services, and never share credentials over chat or email.

## Incident reporting

Report a suspected security incident immediately; do not wait until you confirm the cause. Contact the security team through the incident channel or the twenty-four hour hotline. Early reports limit damage, and nobody is penalized for a false alarm.

## Phishing

Treat unexpected links and attachments with suspicion, especially messages that create urgency or ask for credentials. Use the report button in the mail client to forward suspicious messages to the security team for analysis.

## Device encryption

All laptops and phones that access company data must use full-disk encryption and an automatic screen lock of five minutes or less. Lost or stolen devices must be reported within one hour so that they can be remotely wiped.

## Access control

Access follows the principle of least privilege. Request only the permissions your role needs, and managers review team access every quarter. Access is removed on the last day of employment, and contractor accounts expire automatically after ninety days.

## Data classification

Data is labeled public, internal, confidential, or restricted. Confidential and restricted data must be encrypted in transit and at rest, and may not be copied to personal devices or unapproved cloud storage.

## Vendor security review

Any third-party service that will process customer data needs a security review before purchase. The review checks certifications, breach history, and data residency, and the outcome is recorded in the vendor register.
